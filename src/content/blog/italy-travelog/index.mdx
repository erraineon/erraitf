I was born and grew up in Italy, but after moving to Boston in 2019, I hadn't returned in over seven years. Some things had changed, and others I had never taken note of.

Beyond the cliché facts about Italy (or, more specifically, Rome) that everybody knows, here's a list of observations that surprised even a native like myself.

## Nature

<FloatingFigure image={dawn} alt="Dawn">
  A field west of Rome at 6:30 AM.
</FloatingFigure>

1. There are a lot of lizards.
1. Dawn and sunset feel more intensely orange.
1. There are far more green parakeets than I remember (my boyfriend tells me they are ring-necked parrots and monk parrots).
1. There are many stray cats, but no stray dogs.
1. Growing citrus and fruits in your yard is common. Lots of lemons.
1. Pinecones are much larger and pine nuts can be easily sourced.
1. The sand is soft, at least compared to East Coast beaches.
1. Prickly pear grows everywhere but it's not that commonly eaten.

## Lifestyle

<FloatingFigure image={talenti} alt="Talenti">
  Talenti, the neighborhood I grew up in.
</FloatingFigure>

9. Houses are mostly made of brick and stucco. Wooden houses are rare.
1. Because prices are tax-inclusive and rounded, exact change is very convenient. Many items cost exactly 1 or 2 euros.
1. Tourist spots are noticeably geared towards Russians. A lot of the foreign signage is exclusively in cyrillic.
1. People dress more stylishly on average.
1. People know each other. You constantly hear people saying heyyy how've you been on the streets.
1. People in Rome treat you more affably if you speak in a Roman accent. Which, all things considered, isn't that surprising.
1. Almost every business takes a lunch break from 1 to 3 PM.
1. Drivers won't stop but rather drive around you on the crosswalk.
1. Drivers are more impatient and will frequently pass you on one-lane roads if you're going below or at the speed limit.
1. There are tons of motor scooters, since they're cheaper and easier to maneuver and park in the city.
1. There are notably more old people outside of tourist areas.
   <FloatingFigure image={edicola} alt="Edicola">
     Newsstand, or *edicola*. I used to buy Pokémon cards here.
   </FloatingFigure>
1. There are many newsstands that are still in business, surprisingly.
1. Eating at sit-down restaurants is more of a ritual. You will be there for a while.
1. There are a lot of radio antennas, even if they're likely no longer in use.
1. Indoor air conditioners aren't as common as you'd think.
1. Finding good quality, solid wood furniture is easy and cheap.
1. Not as many people speak English as you'd expect.

## Food

<FloatingFigure image={pastries} alt="Pastries">
  This box of artisanal pastries cost around $22.
</FloatingFigure>

26. There is mostly no Diet Coke, only Coke Zero. They have tiny 150ml cans as opposed to the 222ml mini ones from the US. My boyfriend loved them.
1. Alcohol is sold just about anywhere. A cheap bottle of wine will set you back around five bucks.
1. When they say coffee, they mean espresso. "Coffee in a big cup" is an Americano. Most people don't seem to know what drip, pour-over, or french press are.
1. Even a cheap espresso from a random cafe tastes better than many coffee shops in the US.
1. Many cafes make their own pastries rather than sourcing them from local bakeries.
1. Canteloupe tastes sweeter.
1. Mozzarella tastes milkier.
1. Milk only lasts a couple of days and tastes creamier.
1. Homemade bread tastes more rustic, with a sour tang like sourdough.
1. In general, a lot of the base ingredients taste better.
1. You can get a beer at McDonalds. The ordering screen just asks if you're 18+ and you press yes.
1. Sides are rarely mixed with the mains on the same plate, and rather presented on their own dishes.
