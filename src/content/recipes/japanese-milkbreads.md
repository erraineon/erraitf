## Ingredients

### Tangzhong

- 43 grams of water
- 43 grams of milk
- 14 grams of bread flour

### Dough

- 300 grams of bread flour
- 14 grams of dry milk
- 50 grams of sugar
- 6 grams of salt
- 1 tablespoon of instant yeast
- 113 grams of milk
- 1 large egg
- 57 grams unsalted butter, melted

### For egg wash

- 1 large egg beaten with 1 tablespoon of water

## Instructions

1. For the tangzhong, whisk all tangzhong ingredients in a saucepan on medium low heat until no lumps remain, whisking constantly until the thickened mixture leaves lines on the bottom of the pan. (About 3 to 5 minutes)
2. Transfer the tangzhong to the stand mixer bowl. Add cold milk, flour and remaining ingredients in the order listed.
3. Mix on low speed with the dough hook. Knead the dough until it's smooth, 10 to 12 minutes on medium-low speed.[^1]
4. Shape the dough into a ball, and let it rest in a lightly greased covered bowl for 1 to 1 1/2 hours, until puffy.
5. Deflate the dough, divide it into eight to ten pieces, and shape each piece into a ball.
6. Place the rolls into a lightly greased round pan, cover it, and let them rest for 40 to 50 minutes, until puffy.
7. Preheat the oven to 350°F with a rack in the center.
8. Brush the rolls with the egg wash, and bake for 25 to 30 minutes, until golden brown. A thermometer in the center of the middle roll should read at least 190°F.
9. Remove from the oven and let them cool for 10 minutes.
10. Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days.

[^1]: The dough has developed enough gluten when you do the windowpane test. Take a golfball sized ball of the dough and stretch it up to light. If you can see light through it without the dough tearing, it is ready to rest.
